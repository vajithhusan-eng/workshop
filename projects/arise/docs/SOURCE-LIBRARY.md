## 1. Video — anime/genre analysis

| Channel / format | Why it matters | What Arise takes |
|---|---|---|
| **Gigguk** (YouTube, ~3.6 M) | The widest-audience anime criticism; tracks what the fandom actually feels about a show and *why* it lands or doesn't | Vocabulary and honesty about the genre's pleasures — we can name the fantasy instead of hiding it |
| **Super Eyepatch Wolf** (YouTube) | Long-form essays on why a work *works*: wrestling, manga, horror, the psychology of a fandom | The essay structure we use in this very doc set: premise → mechanic → emotional engine |
| **Mother's Basement** (YouTube) | Structural/technical analysis of openings, pacing, adaptation | The idea that **format** carries meaning: a status window is a format choice |
| **Trash Taste** (podcast/video) | Three anime-adjacent hosts on taste, authenticity and why some things age badly | The reminder that the audience can smell a cash-in; sincerity is the moat |
| Genre explainers & trope catalogues | Taxonomies of system/regression/returner manhwa | The two-axis grammar in `GENRE-ATLAS.md` §1 |

**How to use this section:** these are *taste calibration*, not requirements.
They tell us what the audience already recognises, which is what a reference has
to get right before it can subvert anything.

## 3. Books — game design

| Book | Author | What Arise takes |
|---|---|---|
| **The Art of Game Design** | Jesse Schell | The lens framework: a game is *experience first*. Every System screen is designed from the player's feeling backwards |
| **Reality Is Broken** | Jane McGonigal | Why games satisfy where life doesn't: clear goals, voluntary obstacles, feedback. The honest version of "make life a game" |
| **Rules of Play** | Salen & Zimmerman | Games as systems of rules; the meaning is in the constraint | 
| **Play** | Stuart Brown | Play is not frivolous — it is how mammals rehearse survival. Justifies the game layer as serious |
| **Flow** | Csikszentmihalyi | Challenge must track skill. The reason a rank-up must feel *earned* |
| **Actionable Gamification** (Octalysis) | Yu-kai Chou | Eight core drives. We use the *intrinsic* four (meaning, empowerment, mastery, creativity) and refuse the *black-hat* four (scarcity, unpredictability, avoidance, social pressure) |
| **The Gamification of Learning and Instruction** | Karl Kapp | Where points/badges genuinely help: recognition of a *capability*, not a click |
| **Hooked** | Nir Eyal | Read as a **threat model**, not a playbook. Its Hook cycle is exactly what a private discipline app must not become |

## 5. Research papers

| Paper | Why it is here | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Deci & Ryan — Self-Determination Theory** | Autonomy, competence, relatedness are the nutrients of durable motivation | Quests are user-authored; competence is shown, not granted; "relatedness" is served by the user's real relationships, not a social feed |
| **Deci, Koestner & Ryan (1999)** — rewards and intrinsic motivation meta-analysis | Tangible rewards can *undermine* intrinsic motivation | No XP for things the user already loves; never pay for the behaviour we want to last |
| **Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski (2006)** — PENS | Games satisfy SDT needs; that is why they hold attention | The System must satisfy competence and autonomy, never only pressure |
| **Hamari, Koivisto & Sarsa (2014)** — *Does Gamification Work?* | The foundational review: effects are real but **conditional and uneven** | Gamification is not a feature you add; it is a fit you design |
| **Li, Hew & Du (2024)** — gamification meta-analysis, 35 interventions, ~2 500 participants | Hedges' g ≈ 0.26 on intrinsic motivation; autonomy and relatedness up, **competence barely moves** | Our differentiator must be *competence feedback* — the thing generic gamification fails at. Arise shows what you can now do |
| **Xi & Hamari (2019)** — does gamification satisfy needs? | Points/badges only work when they map to a need | Every badge must name a capability |
| **Toda et al. (2018)** — *The Dark Side of Gamification* | A taxonomy of game elements that harm users | Cross-checked against our 12 red lines |
| **Gray et al. (2018)** — dark patterns in UX | How interfaces coerce | The notification and copy rules in `GAMIFICATION-ETHICS.md` |
| **Ian Bogost (2011)** — *Gamification is Bullshit* | The sharpest critique: gamification often replaces substance with points | Read before every feature: *is this the mechanic or the substance?* |
| **Nicholson (2015)** — RECIPE for meaningful gamification | Meaning over mechanics: reflection, exposition, choice, play, engagement | The daily log and reflection prompts |
| **Lally et al. (2010)**; **Fournier et al. (2016/17)**; **Gollwitzer (1999)**; **Harkin et al. (2016)**; **Wood (2024)** | Formation timelines, morning/evening asymmetry, implementation intentions, self-monitoring, context levers | See `HABITS-ROUTINES-DEEP.md` §3–§9 |
| **WCAG 2.2** (W3C) | The accessibility floor | Tier C parity, reduced motion, contrast — `3D-IMMERSIVE-UI.md` §9 |

## 6. The world-class bar

What "world top app" has to mean *operationally*, not as a slogan.

### 6.1 What award-winning apps actually do

From the 2026 Apple Design Award cohort (12 apps and games across six categories,
including *Delight & Fun* and *Visuals & Graphics*) the transferable patterns are:

1. **Organise around user intent, not data structures.** The user thinks "I need to
   do today's thing", not "I need the quest table".
2. **Strong hierarchy makes dense information scannable.** One focal point per
   screen — the same rule as the 3D depth system.
3. **Interactions should mirror real-world expectations.** No tutorial needed when
   the gesture is obvious.
4. **Remove barriers to participation.** The first quest must be completable in
   under two minutes with no setup.
5. **Personalisation increases relevance** — but *self-authored*, not
   algorithmically profiled (this app is private; profiling is a red line).
6. **Delight is a discipline**, not a coat of paint: one memorable moment, executed
   perfectly, beats ten effects.

### 6.2 The two traps on the way to "world class"

- **The PBL fallacy** (points, badges, leaderboards). A 122-day streak banner does
  not make someone open the app. If the only engagement is the counter, the product
  is a counter. **Arise's claim to quality is that it shows what the user can now
  do** — the competence dimension generic gamification neglects.
- **The content trap.** More screens, more stats, more dashboards. Every added
  surface is a decision the user has to make. A top app is *smaller* than a
  mediocre one.

### 6.3 Our definition of done for "world class"

| Dimension | Bar |
|---|---|
| **Feel** | Every animation within budget (`3D-IMMERSIVE-UI.md` §7); zero jank on a mid-range Android |
| **Fairness** | XP equals effort, provably; the ledger is monotonic and auditable |
| **Honesty** | No dark pattern from the 12 red lines, checked in review, not just intention |
| **Privacy** | Airplane-mode complete; nothing leaves the device; no telemetry |
| **Accessibility** | Tier C is a first-class experience; WCAG 2.2 AA verified |
| **Restraint** | The v1 scope does not grow. "World top" is depth, not surface area |
| **Durability** | It is still useful when the novelty is gone — the only test that matters |
