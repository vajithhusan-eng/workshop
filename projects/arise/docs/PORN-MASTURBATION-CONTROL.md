# Porn & Masturbation Compulsion — Deep Research

> The highest-stakes use case. It is what ZFA's most-praised reviewer was
> fighting, and it is where a discipline app can do the most good — or the most
> harm. This doc is deliberately **evidence-graded**: we mark what is
> established, what is plausible, and what is community myth. We do not ship a
> claim we cannot source.
>
> **Read §1 before anything else.** It is the ethical frame that the rest of the
> doc exists to serve.

## 1. The ethical frame — compulsion, not sin

This is the single most important paragraph in the project's research.

- **Masturbation is normal, common, and healthy.** It appears in no diagnostic
  manual as inherently problematic. There is **no frequency threshold** above
  which it becomes disorder (Healthline; Reavis/Intrapsychic; ICD-11 commentary).
  A man who masturbates daily can be healthy; one who masturbates weekly can be
  compulsive. **Frequency is not the measure; loss of control and impairment
  are.**
- **Most people who *think* they are porn-addicted are not, and telling them
  they are causes harm.** The **Moral Incongruence (PPMI) model** (Grubbs et al.,
  2019–2025): self-perceived "porn addiction" is driven largely by the *mismatch
  between use and one's moral/religious values*, plus avoidant coping — not by
  use itself. Religiosity moderates this. The distress is real, but its cause is
  incongruence, not neuropathology.
- **ICD-11 is explicit**: Compulsive Sexual Behaviour Disorder (CSBD) **must not
  be diagnosed** on distress arising from *moral judgement* about sexual
  behaviour that would not otherwise be pathological.
- Therefore our product target is **compulsion**: use that the person cannot
  control, that causes *impairment* (sleep, work, relationships, self-directed
  goals, money, illegal content), sustained over time. Our target is **never**
  frequency, never guilt, never morality.

> **→ Arise's rule for this domain: we help people who are stuck, not people who
> are ashamed. The System must be able to tell the difference, and must never
> manufacture the second kind of user.**

## 2. Is "porn addiction" real? — the contested science, stated honestly

There is a genuine, unresolved scientific dispute. We present both sides; the
app must not pretend it is settled.

**The sceptics (behavioural addictions don't exist):**
- **David Ley** (*The Myth of Sex Addiction*) and **Nicole Prause**: argue there
  are no reliable brain differences between self-reported sex addicts and
  controls; that visual stimuli cannot induce addiction-like brain changes; that
  the "brain science" is post-hoc justification ("valley-girl science"). Prause's
  EEG study found *reduced* LPP amplitude to sexual images in heavy users —
  the *opposite* of the expected addiction pattern.
- **Psychology Today (Ley)**: "the idea that porn can be called addictive is
  done, at least in the halls of sexual science." Behaviour's invited
  commentaries "none argue that porn is addictive."

**The addiction-modelling camp:**
- **STD/neuro review literature** (Hilton, Voon, Kraus, Love et al.): CSBs share
  core features with substance addiction — craving, tolerance, withdrawal,
  continued use despite harm, cue reactivity.
- **Prause's own data cuts both ways**: reduced cue response is consistent with
  **habituation/desensitisation**, which the addiction model predicts.
- **Tinbergen's "supranormal stimulus"**: internet porn is a super-normal
  stimulus — more novelty in 10 minutes than ancestors saw in a lifetime.

**Where the science actually stands (what we may say):**
- **Established**: CSBD is a real ICD-11 diagnosis (impulse-control disorder);
  a **minority** of users (roughly **3.2–16.6%** across 42 countries, the
  International Sex Survey) meet criteria for problematic use; distress and
  impairment are real and clinically significant.
- **Established**: moral incongruence and shame independently generate
  "addiction" symptoms. **This is not a fringe view — it is a major research
  programme.**
- **Plausible, not proven**: a dopamine/novelty-driven neuroadaptive process
  underlies severe cases.
- **Not proven / myth**: that porn *causes* erectile dysfunction; that "porn
  addiction" is clinically identical to heroin addiction; that a specific number
  of days causes addiction.
- **DSM-5-TR does not list it**; **ICD-11 does** (as CSBD). We side with
  "compulsive sexual behaviour is real; porn 'addiction' as a brain disease is
  contested."

> **→ Arise: never assert "you are addicted." Offer "here's an evidence-based
> way to change a behaviour that's hurting you, if it is."**

## 3. The neuroscience model (the mechanism we design *with*, not *as fact*)

The most-cited lay model, from **Gary Wilson** (*Your Brain on Porn*) and the
TEDx talks:

```
excess consumption → excess dopamine → ΔFosB accumulation
   → (a) numbed pleasure response  + (b) hyper-reactivity to porn
   → bingeing + craving cycle → escalating novelty
```

Supporting mechanics:
- **Reward prediction error (Schultz)**: dopamine encodes *surprise*, and
  internet porn delivers endless novelty — the engine the model leans on.
- **Cue reactivity**: environment (bedroom, phone, boredom, stress) becomes a
  conditioned trigger.
- **Habituation/desensitisation**: the same content stops satisfying; novelty,
  escalation, and edging follow. (This is the phenomenon both camps accept —
  they differ on whether it is "addiction".)
- **Pleasure–pain balance (Anna Lembke, *Dopamine Nation*)**: every dopamine
  spike is followed by an equal-and-opposite dip below baseline; the "gremlins on
  the pain side" keep the balance tipped toward pain until baseline restores.
  Abstinence re-sets it. **~80% of people feel measurably better after ~30 days.**

## 4. PIED — porn-induced erectile dysfunction (evidence-graded)

- **Claimed**: heavy porn use desensitises men to real partners → difficulty
  with partnered sex, easier solo with porn.
- **Evidence**: mostly **case reports and surveys**, not controlled trials.
  - Kinsey-adjacent researchers first described it (2007).
  - Surveys link heavy use to lower arousal with partners and greater need for
    porn fantasy.
  - Voon et al. (2014): reduced ventral-striatum activation to sexual images in
    compulsive users (consistent with desensitisation).
  - ~85% of ED in men under 40 is at least partly psychogenic.
- **Counter-evidence**: some studies find *more* porn time → *greater*
  responsiveness to a partner; some conclude data "do not support" the link.
- **Verdict**: **plausible and widely reported, causation unproven.** We may
  describe it as "some men report this and it often improves with abstinence" —
  never as a certainty, never as a scare tactic.

## 5. The recovery mechanics — reboot, flatline, withdrawal

The community's operational model (NoFap, Reboot Nation, Your Brain on Porn):

- **"Reboot"**: abstain from porn (and often masturbation/orgasm — "PMO") for a
  period — typically **7–90 days** — to "unhook" from artificial stimulation and
  re-sensitise to real intimacy.
- **Withdrawal / "flatline"**: a phase of near-zero libido, low mood, low
  motivation, brain fog. Community-reported onset **~days 8–14**, lasting up to
  **~2–6 weeks**. Most debilitating claim: it makes people panic and break the
  streak to "test" that everything still works (**the "testing" trap**).
- **Honest status**: the *flatline is not established in peer-reviewed
  research* — NoFap itself says "we have yet to see any peer-reviewed academic
  research on it." It is a community phenomenon, plausible as withdrawal or
  conditioning, but unproven.
- **`~64.2%`** of one sample had tried masturbation abstinence at least once.

> **→ Arise: the flatline must be *pre-warned* — a System message on ~day 8:
> "Libido may drop. This is expected, not damage. The testing trap breaks more
> streaks than desire does."** This single in-app message may be the highest-value
> thing we build for this community.

## 11. What this means for Arise — concrete design

**The quest template** (a "bad habit" quest, HRT-shaped):
- **Awareness**: "What was happening 30 seconds before the urge?" → trigger map.
- **Competing response**: a named replacement action (walk, cold water, breath).
- **Urge Surf** tool: name it, watch the wave (~10 min timer), log resistance or
  a lapse without judgement.
- **Day counter + honest milestone rewards** (hidden-until-earned, per ZFA's
  "you can't see the badges until you've reached the days").

**The relapse flow** (AVE-safe — the most important UX in the app):
- A lapse is a **"stumble"**, never a "failure" or a streak wipe.
- Copy attributes to **situation/trigger**, never to the self.
- Immediately offers a **Recovery Quest** and a **Fresh Start** reset
  (psychological segregation from the past self).
- Never resets the *total* — the record of all attempts is kept and honoured.

**The three guardrails specific to this domain:**
1. **Screen for impairment, not frequency or guilt.** Onboarding asks about
   control and life-impact — never "how often" or "do you feel bad."
2. **Build the off-ramp.** If the user reports no impairment and only
   moral/religious distress, the app says so honestly and points to the right
   kind of help (not more tracking). This is the anti-shame feature, and it is
   the ethical core of the whole product.
3. **Always signpost professional help.** The System is a supplement. Say it in
   the UI, on the store listing, and in onboarding.

**Guardrails we inherit from `PSYCHOLOGY.md` / `GAMIFICATION-ETHICS.md`:**
no shame copy, no fake progress, no real-money stakes, no moralising, no
religious coercion, no vulnerability profiling, on-device only.

## 12. Honest summary — what we assert vs won't

**We assert:** compulsion is real and treatable; distress and impairment (not
frequency or guilt) are the target; the dopamine/novelty mechanism is plausible;
the flatline is a community phenomenon worth pre-warning; community +
accountability + skills help; **the app is a supplement, not a cure.**

**We will NOT assert:** "you are addicted"; a specific day count causes addiction;
porn *causes* ED; NoFap delivers superpowers or guaranteed testosterone spikes;
masturbation is wrong; morality is the point.
